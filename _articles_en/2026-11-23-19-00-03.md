Many people moving to Nottingham feel they are starting from zero in the UK job market. Yet experience gained in Hong Kong — careful administration, responsive customer service or coordination across teams — can contain valuable transferable skills.

The key is perspective-taking: explaining your professional story from the employer's point of view. A change of setting does not erase what you have learned. It gives you a reason to describe that value more clearly.

## Make your experience specific

### Administration and management: show how you helped things work better

Go beyond “handled paperwork”. Explain how you organised complex information, coordinated a team, improved a process or learned a new system. If you are learning about UK data protection or payroll, describe your progress honestly. Experience in Hong Kong should not be presented as existing expertise in UK regulations.

### Front-line service: show listening and judgement

Retail, hospitality and customer service involve far more than selling. Patience, empathy, active listening and adaptability become convincing through real examples: handling a complaint, clarifying a customer's needs, or maintaining service under pressure.

### Education and training: explain how you understood different needs

Education and social care often involve understanding people from different backgrounds and adapting how you communicate. These examples can help an employer recognise cross-cultural communication, community coordination and support skills. Match them to the actual requirements of the role.

## A STAR checklist

- **Situation:** Explain the background and the challenge.
- **Task:** Identify your responsibility, the goal and why it mattered.
- **Action:** Give this the most attention. Use clear verbs such as coordinated, improved, resolved or established. Explain your own contribution and show listening, empathy or communication through what you did.
- **Result:** Describe the actual impact and what you learned. Include reliable figures when you have them; otherwise use a clear description. Never invent numbers to improve a story.

One honest example is more persuasive than a list of professional terms. Choose a situation you know well, then ask: which ability will this help an employer see?
