Snow can disrupt school, work and everyday arrangements. A simple household plan can make those changes easier to manage.

This article is adapted from the newsletter of 8 January 2026. Its weather forecast and cancellation notice have expired and should not be used to judge conditions today.

## Check current information

- Read [Met Office weather advice and preparation guidance](https://weather.metoffice.gov.uk/warnings-and-advice/weatherready/top-tips-to-be-weatherready).
- Watch for messages directly from school, including texts, emails and services such as ClassDojo, ParentMail or Arbor. Schools do not necessarily make closure decisions at the same time.
- Check your bus, tram or rail operator before travelling, and allow extra time.
- Discuss who could help if school closes or transport is disrupted. Explain possible changes in advance to children who rely on familiar routines.

## Prepare for everyday needs

Check everyday food, essential medication, warm clothing and usable lighting. If you are concerned about heating or pipes, ask the supplier or a qualified professional for advice suited to your home, rather than relying on a general thermostat setting in an old newsletter.

## Take care on paths and steps

Ice is not always easy to see. Pay particular attention to steps and slopes. Before clearing snow yourself, read [GOV.UK's guidance](https://www.gov.uk/clear-snow-road-path-cycleway): do not pour water over snow, because it can freeze again. Follow local rules for public grit bins rather than treating public supplies as private materials.

Use the appropriate emergency or public service when help is needed. Saving current contact information is more reliable than keeping an old newsletter's list of numbers.
