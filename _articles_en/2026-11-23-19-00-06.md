When preparing for an interview, we often spend a great deal of time practising English answers and the STAR method. Yet communication is already happening as we enter the room. Listening, expression, posture and responses all contribute to how a conversation feels.

A recruitment guest at Steps Towards Work reminded us that an interview is both an exploration of skills and experience, and a chance to establish a professional connection.

## Give yourself room to relax

Worrying about English or unfamiliar workplace expectations can make someone feel tense. In practice, notice whether you give yourself time to hear the question and whether a natural smile, nod or gesture helps you respond.

These are options for communication. People have different cultural backgrounds, physical needs and ways of expressing themselves. You do not have to force a particular expression or level of eye contact. Body language alone cannot establish someone's competence or honesty.

## Find a comfortable, engaged posture

Sit in a way that lets you listen and answer without becoming rigid. Eye contact can be natural rather than constant; if looking directly into someone's eyes is uncomfortable, find another way to show attention.

If you need time to think, pause. You can ask for a question to be repeated or clarified before organising your answer. Communicating your needs matters more than forcing an immediate response.

## Rebuild confidence through practice

A temporary job does not erase your previous professional identity. Interview practice can help you organise your experience and feel more confident expressing it.

Try taking turns asking questions with someone you trust. Focus feedback on whether you understood the question, gave a specific example and spoke at a comfortable pace. Make one small adjustment, then try again.

Interviewers are people too. Prepare honest examples while allowing the conversation to feel natural, instead of aiming for a perfect set of gestures.
