AI is changing the world of work, prompting familiar questions: which jobs will remain safe, and what should we learn next?

Issue 13 of Steps Towards Work used a Wall Street Journal careers article as a starting point for reflection. This version retains the newsletter's discussion. It is neither a guarantee about an industry's future nor a forecast of today's labour market.

## Start with your interests and abilities

Ask what you enjoy, what you do well and how those abilities help other people. Understanding your starting point can be more useful than continually chasing the next popular role.

## Prepare for changing tasks

Change may arrive through new tools, processes and ways of working, rather than the sudden disappearance of a whole job. Knowing a tool can help; so can understanding, relearning and adapting.

## Combine your strengths

Communication and analysis, or technical knowledge and interpersonal skills, can support each other. When reviewing your experience, consider how your abilities work together, rather than only whether you know the latest software.

## Keep judgement and responsibility

AI can organise information or generate material. People using it still need to check facts, understand limitations and take responsibility for decisions. Recognising uncertainty and knowing when to investigate further are useful abilities too.

## Keep building trust and connection

Helping people feel able to trust and work with you takes listening, time and real interaction. Those experiences deserve a place in your development plans.

You do not have to decide on an unchanging career today. Choose one practical step: learn a relevant skill, speak with someone in the field, or organise a real example that shows what you can do.
