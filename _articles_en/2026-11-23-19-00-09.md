Moving to the UK often means juggling work, learning and everyday adjustments. One useful community resource may already be close by: the public library.

Libraries offer more than physical books. They can be a starting point for learning, working on a CV and getting to know your community.

## What can membership offer?

**Digital books and audio.** Inspire in Nottinghamshire and Nottingham City Libraries both offer digital reading. Inspire currently lists BorrowBox; City Libraries offers BorrowBox and other digital resources. English audiobooks or a subject you enjoy can help you develop a regular reading and listening habit. Apps, publications and catalogues vary between services, so check your library's current offer.

- [Inspire e-reading](https://www.inspireculture.org.uk/reading-information/reading/ereading/ebooks/)
- [Nottingham City Libraries Digital Reads](https://www.nottinghamcitylibraries.co.uk/our-services/digitalreads/)

**Computers, Wi-Fi and space to work.** Ask your local library about somewhere to edit your CV or prepare for an interview. Printing usually has a separate charge; check computer availability, session limits and booking arrangements too.

## Beeston Library: support and connection

Beeston Library's official page currently lists a Tuesday Job Club, 10am to noon, for help with job searching and CVs. It also describes free Wi-Fi and computer use.

The original newsletter highlighted chess, children's rhyme sessions and other gatherings. These can offer ways to meet people locally. Before visiting, check [Beeston Library's official page](https://www.inspireculture.org.uk/reading-information/find-a-library/beeston-library/) for current dates, participation arrangements and any charges.

## Find your library service

Libraries in Broxtowe, including Beeston, Stapleford and Chilwell, are part of Inspire's Nottinghamshire network. For libraries within Nottingham city, start with [Nottingham City Libraries](https://www.nottinghamcitylibraries.co.uk/).

Each service has its own membership and digital arrangements. Read the current joining information or ask staff which documents are needed, rather than relying on an old newsletter's list.

If you are unsure where to begin, try one small step: visit your local library and say, “I'd like to join the library.” You may find more than a book — perhaps a place to become more comfortable in your new community.
