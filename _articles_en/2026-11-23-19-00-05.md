Starting a job search again can feel stressful. These reminders first appeared in January, but can offer a starting point at any time of year.

## 1. Starting again does not mean you have failed

Not finding work during the previous stretch does not mean you are behind. Give yourself room to update your CV, consider your direction and choose a next step.

## 2. Watch for vacancies at a pace you can sustain

Recruitment schedules vary between employers. Check suitable roles regularly, without feeling pushed by claims about a “new-year hiring season” or waiting for a particular month.

## 3. UK experience can include more than paid work

Volunteering, schools, charity shops and community activities may provide examples of working and communicating locally. Even an hour or two a week can help build confidence. Whether it meets a particular role's experience requirements depends on the employer.

## 4. Explain Hong Kong experience in clear workplace language

Go beyond translating a job title. Describe your actual responsibilities, such as administrative coordination or front-line customer service, while keeping your position and duties accurate.

## 5. Recognise transferable skills

Punctuality, communication, teamwork and reliability become visible through examples. Caring for family, church service and community activities may also offer relevant evidence.

## 6. You do not have to wait for perfect English

Check the language skills a role needs, then consider applying. You can honestly say: “English is my second language, and I am learning and practising.” Keep improving in ways that meet your needs.

## 7. Practise interviews somewhere supportive

Work through questions with someone you trust, learn about interview formats and allow yourself to make mistakes. You can also ask Steps Towards Work about current support.

## 8. Aim for thoughtful applications

Sending large numbers of applications can be exhausting. Try a manageable starting target, perhaps two to four a week, and adapt your CV after understanding each role. Adjust the number to your circumstances.

## 9. Consider travel alongside everyday responsibilities

Nearby work may be easier to fit into your life, but proximity does not guarantee flexibility. Explore community centres, schools, charity shops, councils or GP practices, and check each role's hours and requirements.

## 10. You do not have to face this alone

Looking for work in a new country brings real emotional pressure. Asking trusted friends, peers or employment support services for help is also a way of looking after yourself.
