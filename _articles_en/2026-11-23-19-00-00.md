Moving to the UK may mean leaving a familiar job title behind. It does not wipe out the experience you have built over ten or twenty years.

Often, the difficulty is not a lack of ability. We are simply used to introducing ourselves through our previous job titles, without stopping to explain how we actually got things done.

Take management experience. Saying “I used to lead a team” can feel rather abstract to a UK employer. What makes it convincing is the strength behind it: how you spotted problems, set priorities with limited resources, or brought different opinions together. Those abilities can travel with you, even when your surroundings change.

## Spend 15 minutes a week rediscovering your strengths

Try a small habit: once a week, write down one situation you handled. You do not need a polished CV paragraph or an impressive achievement. Just ask yourself three honest questions.

**What did you notice?** What was happening? Why did you decide to deal with this issue before another? Decisions that feel obvious to you may reveal observation and judgement.

**What steps did you take?** Break the process into two or three actions. If colleagues disagreed, perhaps you listened to both sides, returned to the shared goal, and worked out a way forward together. That is a real example, rather than a vague claim that you are “good at communication”.

**Where else could you use that ability?** This can be the most revealing question. Customer service may have developed your listening and empathy; project work may have strengthened coordination and systems thinking. Even if you are currently working in a warehouse, shop or restaurant, those strengths can still help in a new setting.

## You can start before finding a job

You do not have to wait for your ideal role. Alongside memories of work in Hong Kong, record recent experiences in the UK: arranging school or healthcare for family members, volunteering, or learning an unfamiliar system. Life experience cannot fully replace professional experience, but it can show resilience and an ability to learn.

Nor must every entry be a success story. When something was frustrating or did not go as planned, looking back at how you responded and what you learned can be just as valuable.

Over time, you may notice strengths appearing again and again. Whether you are writing an application, preparing for an interview or exploring a new direction, you will have more confidence in your evidence. Starting again does not mean leaving your past self behind.
