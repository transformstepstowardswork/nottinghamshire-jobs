A few days ago, I watched an interview with Louis Cheung. Something he said made me stop and think. I wanted to share that feeling, and a little of my own experience of looking for work in the UK.

Two weeks after arriving, I started sending out CVs. I worked on them day and night, sending more than twenty applications a week, but my inbox stayed silent. After two months, I finally had my first interview. Then came the phone call: “I’m sorry to let you know…”

I kept trying, and kept being turned down. This went on for six months.

One day, I felt I had had enough. I stopped searching for those roles and took agency work in factories and warehouses, giving myself some breathing space. A few months later, I asked myself whether I really wanted to leave it there.

So I started again, more slowly this time, applying only for jobs I truly wanted. Two months later, I joined Transform.

What stayed with me from that interview was the idea that the hardest stretches of life can also be where we grow most, and that what we live through can become something we draw on later.

That really spoke to me.

I hope sharing this can offer you a little of the encouragement it gave me. Keep going, even if you need to go slowly. You are not alone.

*Anthony Kan — personal reflection, originally shared in Steps Towards Work, issue 3.*
