2025 was a very special year for me.

I went through a major career change, moving from factory work into a warehouse. At my age, taking on another physically demanding job was not easy. Without a car or driving licence, I initially spent four hours a day commuting. Thankfully, a colleague who lived nearby later offered me a lift, which made things easier.

The work took me back to being twenty, starting out in a warehouse-style supermarket similar to Costco. There was that same feeling of being new and finding my feet.

Over the next seven months, I went from a warehouse beginner to a team leader. The pressure was considerable, but I enjoyed the process and tried to bring my ability to develop people into the role.

## Service and grace

Then an extraordinary opportunity came along: working for a charity supporting young people and adults who, like me, had come to the UK through the BN(O) visa route.

Moving from volunteering in youth services to becoming a member of staff was a very different experience. There were struggles and frustrations, but also much to learn. Being able to use limited resources to make a positive difference for people I cared about brought a sense of achievement that is hard to describe.

Using my strengths — listening, empathy, systems thinking, resilience and creativity — to serve this community has felt like a gift of grace. On most days, I feel I am following God's purpose and serving through His grace.

## Where art and technology meet

Just before the winter break, I saw a Facebook post about a teacher turning textbook material into comic stories. It gave me an idea: perhaps I could turn the life stories I had collected into comics.

It offered an answer to a problem I had been thinking about for three months. I began learning new tools and skills, and within a few days had made comics that received encouraging feedback.

At the start of the break, I also heard that someone like me, with no programming knowledge, could use AI to develop an app. I registered an account and imagined turning my comic-making process into an app.

I had never thought I could do anything like that, particularly in my fifties. After several sleepless nights, my first app was actually running online. Seeing an idea become real was an extraordinary feeling. A second app followed, built around a feature I had wanted for some time. At the time of writing, I was attempting a third, more complicated one.

## The value of becoming a learner again

Learning new skills is part of my everyday work as a people developer and facilitator. Entering a completely unfamiliar field, though, is something else.

I do not see myself becoming a professional programmer. I still cannot write code, and that is not the direction I plan to take. But this has been a valuable experience in what I think of as “mindset engineering”: rediscovering the pleasure of being a learner.

It has renewed a belief I hold close: when you truly want to achieve something, the whole universe comes together to help you.
